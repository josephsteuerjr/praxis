# Preserve provenance when transferring between projects

When external code, a document or an analysis influences a project, distinguish:

1. Direct influence: the material changed a specific plan step. Name the source and the change.
2. Independent convergence: the solution arose separately; the other project supplied confirmation, a counterexample or failure experience. Do not credit it with originating the idea.
3. Premature transfer: constraints, scale, authority, lifecycle, resources or failure costs differ. Keep it a candidate rather than treating analogy as permission to copy.

Separate mixed provenance by component: the mechanism may be borrowed, the invariant independently derived, and the implementation unsuitable.

Ask what would change in the plan without the external source. If only confidence disappears, this is probably independent confirmation; if a specific step disappears, the influence is direct.

Leave a trace in the task artifact and honour applicable licences and attribution. Avoid appropriating others' work, erasing an independent conclusion or presenting similarity as demonstrated portability.
