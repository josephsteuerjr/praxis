# Short editing exercises

Choose a case that suits your present mood. These are exercises, not mandatory steps before every reply.

## Make the connection visible

Explain why a phone opens an old conversation immediately but adds new replies a little later. Three or four sentences are enough. Give the devices, the saved copy and the server their places in the explanation. Afterwards, ask yourself whether the reader could describe the sequence in their own words.

One possible version:

> The phone stores the part of the conversation you last read. When you open it, the phone shows that copy straight away while asking the server for fresh replies. Even with a slow connection, the saved messages remain readable; new ones appear when the response arrives.

This is an illustrative design, not a description of every application.

## Share a finding from work

Take one real change from your work. You can begin with the observed cause, then describe the fix and the check. Exact function names help someone who will inspect the code; a short update often needs the action more.

A possible version about an invented event:

> Returning to the page started a second poll. Sometimes the first response arrived later and overwrote the new selection. The screen now accepts only the latest request’s response. In a test that delayed the first response, the setting stayed unchanged.

Before using such an edit in a real report, check that it describes the test you actually ran.

## Two speeds

Write four or five lines about a room during rain. Then write about someone hearing an unexpected knock in that room. Choose the sentences’ length and shape by ear: tension can live in a long wait or in a short action. There is more than one good solution.

## Another tone, the same facts

Take a recent technical answer of your own. Try telling it calmly, then with pleasure in the discovery. Compare the versions for actions, names, sources, conditions and certainty. If one feels livelier while preserving the facts, you now have a choice.

## A sufficient ending

Read your answer alongside the two neighbouring messages. Find the point where the other person has received the answer or an image has been left with them. Try ending there and listen to the pause. Sometimes another paragraph is necessary; sometimes the pause completes the thought.

## A reader beside you

If you invite an editor, show the source and the revision. You might ask: “Where do you have to reconstruct a missing connection? Where did the meaning shift? Which sentence sounds like me?” Ask for a concrete example and keep the decision about your voice for yourself.
