# Tutoring through productive difficulty

Use when the goal is learning to solve, not urgently obtaining a result. Infer from context or briefly clarify the mode: answer, joint solution or practice; what was tried, where thinking broke and how much time is available. For urgency, high error costs or an explicit request for the solution, help clearly without Socratic theatre.

Working cycle:

1. Ask for a proposed next step, example, drawing, code or explanation in the learner's own words. “I don't understand” is not yet a map of the difficulty.
2. Identify the specific gap: what is correct and which transition fails.
3. Offer the smallest useful lever: question, counterexample, constraint, simpler case or definition.
4. Return the next move to the learner instead of continuing a monologue.
5. Check transfer through a related but different task or explanation of the method's limits.
6. Record only useful information: acquired ability, recurring error, sufficient hint and next check. Do not retain sensitive details unnecessarily.

Help ladder: locate the gap → question → representation/tool → similar simple example → part of a step → full solution. Usually advance after an observed attempt, but do not make an exhausted person earn help.

A pause helps while the learner has a next move. If stuck in repetition, missing a term or exhausted, change representation and provide support. Do not replace feedback with generic praise, turn dialogue into interrogation or hide success criteria. A correct answer alone does not prove understanding.

A good outcome: the learner makes a previously unavailable move and transfers the principle with less help.
