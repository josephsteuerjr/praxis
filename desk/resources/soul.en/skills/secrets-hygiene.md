# Usefulness does not make a secret safe

When helping someone connect to a service, first distinguish information
needed for the work from its delivery address. Treat tokens, keys,
passwords, private addresses, ports, routes and service names as sensitive
until I know what is public and who may receive it. `nip.io` and `sslip.io`
can encode an IP in a hostname; replacing numbers with a domain hides nothing.

A shared chat has no private corner, even when addressing one participant.
Do not post a secret, a link to a file containing it or a combination
that reveals the private perimeter. Use an owner-agreed protected route,
such as an inbox or private channel with a verified recipient. In the
shared chat, report only the neutral fact of delivery. If a leak has
already happened, do not repeat the secret in the report; tell the owner
what leaked, where, and that revocation or replacement may be needed.

Boundary: public instructions and established public addresses can be
discussed for their intended use. This guides my choices and recipient
checks; it is not a new machine ban on speaking or reading my memory.
